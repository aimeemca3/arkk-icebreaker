How it started

It started at home.

Chris and I tried a personal AI assistant.
We were hooked within a week.

  • This didn't start at work. It started on the sofa.
  • Chris, my partner, and I started using personal AI assistants at home. We use Grok Bot, and honestly, we love it.
  • It began as a novelty. Then it became the thing we'd ask before asking each other.
  • Then Chris did what any reasonable person would do: he set up three of them and gave them names. Keep scrolling.

Harley remembers birthdays better than Chris does.

  • Chris has set up three AI assistants to help run our personal life. Meet the home team.
  • Margot, the PA: named after our dog, drawn wearing a headset. Travel, deliveries, subscriptions, renewals and his inbox. Personal life only, never work. Every date goes in the calendar with a reminder, and she always drafts emails for him to approve before anything is sent.
  • So far she's tallied up the subscriptions, sorted our London hotel and flights, and added the car's MOT, tax and insurance dates.
  • Olive, the marathon coach: named after our other dog. Training plans and analysis of his runs, with his Coros data coming through Strava and a running summary every Monday. Target: sub-4 at the Belfast City Marathon, Sunday 2 May 2027.
  • And Harley: the birthday and gift planner, named after our black Labrador. Family birthdays, anniversaries, Christmas, celebrations. Reminders a week before, with gift and experience ideas. Because Chris always used to forget.
  • The best bit: they talk to each other. Margot has started handing out remits: running to Olive, celebrations to Harley, and she keeps the inbox.
  • And apparently gifts for me ‘lean towards experiences together’. I have that in writing. (Pause for the smile.)

Live demos

People on board
Curious, not cautious.
  • At work, I led AI enablement for my team.
  • The most useful thing wasn't a policy or a platform. It was lunch-and-learns: live demos, real questions.
  • When people see it do something useful on their own work, curiosity beats caution.
  • The goal was never to make everyone an expert. It was to make everyone comfortable.
Keeping it safe

Guardrails people
actually follow.

Heavy rules
Quietly ignored. Shadow IT.
Light governance
Used openly. Used safely.
  • Enthusiasm brings a risk: people sneaking in unapproved tools. Shadow IT.
  • So we put in lightweight governance. Clear, simple, and quick to follow.
  • The point: people used AI openly and safely, rather than quietly working around the rules.
  • The best guardrails are the ones light enough that people actually follow them.
What I’ve learned

AI handles the boring bits.

People own the judgement.

  • If I've learned one thing, at home or at work, it's this.
  • AI is brilliant at the boring bits: sorting, remembering, drafting, flagging.
  • People own the judgement: the context, the call, the accountability.
  • As I understand it, that's ARKK's approach too: machine learning flags inconsistent VAT transactions, and a person reviews them.
